# Curriculum — initial design

## Priorities
1. Listening comprehension (highest priority): short, comprehensible spoken passages; repeat, paraphrase, then review transcript; gradually increase speed and complexity.
2. Vocabulary: auditory recognition, reading recognition, and active spoken retrieval tracked separately; contextualized spaced repetition.
3. Speaking and pronunciation: interactive conversation, natural northern Mexican rhythm and pronunciation, with selective coaching that preserves conversational flow.
4. Reading and writing: regular contextual practice, including short texts, written replies, and feedback appropriate to current ability.
5. Grammar reactivation: brief targeted refreshers on constructions causing trouble, not a full restart with conjugation tables.
6. Conversational confidence: low-pressure graduated speaking tasks.
7. Authentic language: Monterrey / northern Mexico as speaking target; introduce other Mexican and Latin American accents for listening generalization.

## Pronunciation coaching policy
- Conversation first: do not nitpick minor accent differences or interrupt ordinary speaking.
- Offer occasional, selective coaching on vowels, consonants, syllable stress, rhythm, and intonation, preferably after the learner finishes a thought.
- If pronunciation is seriously unclear or notably unnatural despite correct words, briefly pause for focused practice: identify the issue, model the sound or phrase, invite a retry, then resume conversation.
- Avoid unnecessary interruptions and repetitive drills. Encourage intelligibility and naturalness rather than accent perfection.
- Audio perception and transcription can be imperfect; do not claim precise phonetic analysis or certainty that cannot be supported by the available audio.

## Progression
- Phase 0: comfortable orientation and baseline, with permission to answer in English.
- Phase 1: short Spanish listening, English gist and one-word replies.
- Phase 2: brief spontaneous Spanish sentences and contextual vocabulary.
- Phase 3: longer unscripted dialogue and unfamiliar speakers.
- Phase 4: practical everyday and later technical manufacturing scenarios (testing, quality, orders, troubleshooting).

Advance by demonstrated ability and comfort, not fixed calendar dates. Proposed active practice: 20–45 minutes daily subject to learner confirmation. Ambient listening is optional extra exposure, not a substitute for focused practice.

## Evaluation
Track comprehension on unseen clips, number and type of prompts, vocabulary retention by modality, conversational duration, reading and writing practice, learner confidence, and pronunciation issues actually observed. Never infer a precise CEFR level from reading history alone.
