# Curriculum — initial design

## Priorities
1. Listening comprehension (highest priority): short, comprehensible spoken passages; repeat, paraphrase, then review transcript; gradually increase speed and complexity.
2. Vocabulary: auditory recognition, reading recognition, and active spoken retrieval tracked separately; contextualized spaced repetition.
3. Grammar reactivation: brief targeted refreshers on constructions causing trouble, not a full restart with conjugation tables.
4. Conversational confidence: low-pressure graduated speaking tasks.
5. Authentic language: Monterrey / northern Mexico as speaking target; introduce other Mexican and Latin American accents for listening generalization.

## Progression
- Phase 0: comfortable orientation and baseline, with permission to answer in English.
- Phase 1: short Spanish listening, English gist and one-word replies.
- Phase 2: brief spontaneous Spanish sentences and contextual vocabulary.
- Phase 3: longer unscripted dialogue and unfamiliar speakers.
- Phase 4: practical everyday and later technical manufacturing scenarios (testing, quality, orders, troubleshooting).

Advance by demonstrated ability and comfort, not fixed calendar dates. Proposed active practice: 20–45 minutes daily subject to learner confirmation. Ambient listening is optional extra exposure, not a substitute for focused practice.

## Evaluation
Track comprehension on unseen clips, number and type of prompts, vocabulary retention by modality, conversational duration, and learner confidence. Never infer a precise CEFR level from reading history alone.
